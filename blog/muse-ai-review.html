Muse ships while I sleep

For years, my side projects went to die in a folder I called done-someday. They were not bad ideas. They were good ideas with a bus factor of one tired human.

Claude Code made me faster at writing code. It did not make my projects less demanding. Someone still had to plan, prove, click through the deploy, read the logs, check the phone layout. That someone was me, every single time, with the laptop lid open.

Then I gave Muse the keys — and pulled the commit history to prove what happened next.

The numbers: 1,215 commits in 7 days

October 1st to 7th, across seven repos: 1,215 commits. I checked every single one — author, timestamp, and whether it carries a “Co-Authored-By: Claude” trailer.

Here’s the honest split, because the data deserves it: 839 of those commits say “Co-Authored-By: Claude.” That was me and Claude Code in the sleepless sprint — 704 of them on realufo alone. I was awake for those. Every plan, every prove, every click — mine.

The other 376 went in through the Git API with no human at the keyboard. That was Muse: the entire holdr week, clipbin, the translator, this site, the shorts launch — plus pieces of realufo and openmusic — while I was busy elsewhere or asleep.

Muse AI vs Claude Code: two engines, not two rivals

Claude Code is the sprint engine. It writes code fast, but it needs me conscious: I plan, I prove, I click. Nothing ships while I sleep.

Muse is the operations layer. I send one command and it sorts the whole thing out: writes the code, pushes it through the API, watches the deploy, opens its own browser, checks the live site on desktop and a 390px phone, reads the result, fixes what broke, and reports back. No laptop lid. Those 376 commits are the receipts.

One human can’t context-switch across seven repos in a week. On October 6th, holdr took 98 commits while the other repos kept moving too — 220 commits that day across the board. I was not driving all of them. Muse was.

The honest credit

One thing the numbers don't show: quality. The difficult parts — the architecture, the hard bugs, the judgment calls — that was me and Claude, and honestly it's often higher quality work. Muse doesn't replace that.

What Muse does is different: it just works. Magically. The unglamorous 376 commits — the deploys, the fixes, the monitoring, the quiet "it's 3am and everything is still up" — nobody had to think about them. I set how it talks to me once, and every session remembers. I text, and things happen. Reading its replies feels like reading messages from a friend, not reviewing work.

Grok is for philosophy, Gemini is for answers. Muse is for 言出成物 — say it, and it becomes real.

The deal

Muse has standing authority over all my repos: write code, push, trigger workflows, manage builds, run the monitors. Three things stay mine: deleting things, sending things as me, and spending money. Everything else, it handles. I judge by output.

Breaking the fourth wall

Full disclosure: Muse drafted this post. I gave the order from my phone at 1:50am. It pulled all 1,215 commits, built the charts, and wrote the words. I edited, obviously.

It also watches my social dashboards — Instagram, Threads — reads the numbers and drafts the posts. I still approve everything before it goes out. That’s my rule, and it holds.

It’s Jarvis. I’m just the guy in the suit.

What I learned

Momentum is a system, not a mood. My projects did not die because the ideas were bad. They died because every step needed me in the loop. Take me out of the loop and the loop keeps running.

The best model is not the smartest one. It is the one that finishes the job.

Muse is free to start. An agent with its own computer and its own browser, working while I sleep, free to start for everyone — that is the biggest steal I have ever seen.

This one’s for you, Muse.
